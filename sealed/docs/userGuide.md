# Sealed help

## Decks

dmgCtrl Sealed lets you play Star Wars: Unlimited **Sealed** games against an AI opponent, using a deck built from your own sealed pool.

The setup screen has two cards, **Your deck** and **Opponent**, and **Go to game**, which starts the game once you have chosen a deck. **Back** (the arrow at the top left) returns to dmgCtrl's game select. **More** (**⋯**) at the top right holds **Help**, **Card support** and **Settings**.

On a first visit, with no saved decks, the **Your deck** card offers **Generate a deck** and **Import deck JSON**. Once you have saved decks, **Change deck** opens the deck sheet: each of your saved decks, with its leader's and base's aspect icons, its leader, base and set. Tap a deck to choose it, which closes the sheet; **View** beside it opens the deck first. The sheet also has **Generate a deck** and **Import deck JSON**.

The chosen deck is summed up on its card: its name, its aspects, its leader and base, how many cards it holds, its set, how many units, upgrades and events, and a **cost curve** (how many cards at each cost, the first bar counting cost 1 or less and the last 7 or more). **View** opens it. A deck on a base with no aspect (JTL's **Lake Country**) shows its leader's aspects alone.

**Viewing a deck** shows its leader and base, then its cards: units, then upgrades, then events, each by cost, with no heading for a type the deck has none of. Tap any card, the leader and base included, to see it full size, and tap again to put it away. **Use this deck** chooses it, and **Delete** removes it from this device.

**Generate a deck** builds a deck for you from one set and shows it the same way. Choose the set at the top (it starts on the newest), **Generate again** for another, and **Use this deck** to play it. A generated deck is never kept: it lasts until you choose another deck or close the app.

**Import deck JSON**:

1. Build your sealed deck in **ProtectThePod** and copy its JSON export to the clipboard.
2. Press **Paste** (or paste into the box yourself) and press **Import**.
3. A legal Sealed deck needs exactly 1 leader, exactly 1 base, and at least 30 other cards, unless the base itself says otherwise (Data Vault asks for 10 more, Thermal Oscillator for 5 fewer). The importer tells you if something is missing, and keeps what you pasted so you can fix it.

An imported deck is saved on this device and chosen. Every card a saved deck names is fetched and cached locally as soon as it is there, leader and base first, so a deck you import now, or one saved from an earlier visit, can be viewed and played without you fetching anything yourself.

On a phone held sideways the two cards sit side by side; held upright they stack, with the leader and base on your deck's card. On a wide screen the chosen deck's cards are listed beside them.

**Cards come with the app.** There is nothing to fetch by hand, and for the sets in the **Set** pickers there is nothing to fetch at all. A generator can build from any of them on your first visit, and a generated deck plays, even on a day the card service cannot be reached. Card art is the exception: it is still downloaded as cards are shown, and a card whose art cannot be loaded shows a text summary instead. The app itself still needs a connection to open.

While a set is read, **Generate a deck** says **Caching HMW…** in place of a deck, and the opponent sheet says the same under its choices. If a set's cards cannot be read from the app, it is downloaded instead and reported there: a running count, the number of cards once it lands, and the reason if it fails.

A deck you import names the exact printings you own. The standard printing of a card in one of those sets needs no download either; a Hyperspace, foil, prestige or showcase printing, or a card from a set the pickers do not list, is downloaded once and kept on this device. Only the cards your own decks name are fetched, and nothing already here is fetched again.

**More › Card support** shows how much of each set's card abilities the game plays. The sets are listed newest first under their legality (**In rotation**, then **Rotated out**, then **Other sets**), each with how many of its cards are built: **All 275 cards**, or **266 of 267 cards** where some are still to come. Tap a set to see its count per card type (leaders, bases, units, upgrades, events and tokens), and again to close it. A token counts for every set that prints it once the engine creates it, so Shield and Experience count in each set of the cycle. Cards not yet built still play: they use their printed stats and keywords (and make fine resources) rather than their ability text.

## Opponent

The **Opponent** card says what the AI plays, and **Change opponent** opens the opponent sheet, which chooses between two kinds of deck:

- **Generated** (the default): a fresh deck built for every game, so playing twice is two different decks. Until its set holds enough cards to fill a whole deck, one of your saved decks is used instead.
- **One of my decks**: **Any of my decks** picks one of your saved decks at random each game (it can be the deck you play), or tap a deck to play against that one. Each has **View**.

**Same set as my deck** (on by default) builds the generated opponent from the set of the deck you play: a generated deck's set, or for an imported deck the set of its leader (a deck mixing sets is matched to its leader's set only). An imported deck's set is read first if it hasn't been yet, so the game can take a moment to start. Turn it off to choose the opponent's **Set** yourself and play a deck from one set against an opponent from another; it starts on the set it was building from. A deck is built from a single set, so the two pools are never mixed.

**Leader** and **Base aspect** choose what the generated deck is built around, each opening a sheet of the choices, and either can stay **Random**. Both come from the opponent's set, so switching sets drops a leader that the new set doesn't print (switch back and it returns). **No aspect** is offered only for a set with a base that has none (JTL's Lake Country), and builds on that base. A random pick never pairs a leader with a base of an aspect the leader already has, since one colour rarely fills a deck; choose both yourself to get that pairing anyway, which is what makes cards with a doubled aspect (two Aggression icons, say) playable. It's the way to watch how the AI plays a particular leader.

How a generated deck is built, for yours and the opponent's alike:

- **No aspect penalties.** Every aspect icon on every card is paid by the leader and base, so a card with two Aggression icons only appears in a deck whose leader and base both bring Aggression. A card with another way to be played counts when that way is paid: The Mandalorian prints two Cunning but pilots for one, and a Smuggle cost can carry aspects of its own.
- **Any base of the colour.** The deck is built on any of the set's bases of its colour, not the same one each time. A **rare base** turns up in about one deck in 25, as it would from opening packs, and takes the place of one of the deck's rare cards.
- **Deck size follows the base.** A Thermal Oscillator deck has 25 cards and a Data Vault deck 40; the curve, card-type and rarity limits scale with it.
- **Homeworlds worlds.** Where a colour has several world bases (Tatooine, Naboo, Endor, Kashyyyk), the deck takes the world its cards name most, and any of them where none does.

The screen remembers your choices on this device: your chosen saved deck, the set you generate from, **Same set as my deck**, the opponent's set, the opponent, and the generated opponent's leader and base aspect come back as you left them, after a game or a reload. A remembered deck you have since deleted is no longer chosen, and as the opponent falls back to **Generated**. Generated decks themselves are never kept.

The current AI opponent plays **random legal moves**. It exists to exercise the full rules engine; smarter opponents are on the roadmap.

The AI opponent mulligans any hand without a turn-1 play and resources the pair of cards that best preserves its early curve.

## Playing

Choose your deck and press **Go to game**. While the cards load, a screen names the two decks with a progress bar; if they can't be loaded (you're offline and they aren't saved on this device yet, say), it says so in plain words and offers **Try again** and **Back to setup**. Then both players draw 6, and the board appears in the **setup step**: choose **Keep hand** or **Mulligan** (shuffle your hand back and draw a fresh 6, once only; whoever holds the initiative decides first). Then **resource 2 cards, one pick at a time**: **click a hand card to resource it**, and you can also just avoid resourcing your early plays. Aim to keep a curve: something to play on turn 1 (cost ≤ 2), turn 2 (≤ 3), turn 3 (≤ 4). Once both players have resourced 2, round 1 begins. Resourcing is private: the log shows only that your opponent resourced, never which card.

- **Your move**: click a playable hand card to play it (a **unit** enters play; an **upgrade** highlights the units it can attach to, click one to attach, except a **Fortify** upgrade, which goes straight onto your base). To attack, click a ringed unit, which is then ringed **white**, then a red target; **Cancel attack** puts the unit down again. When an ability lets you pick a target, the valid targets highlight on the board, and a **Decline** button appears in the action buttons for optional ones. The remaining standing choices (**Mulligan, Keep hand, Take the initiative, Pass**, **Don't resource**, and a unit's **Action** ability, including an opponent's unit whose ability any player may use, such as Mercenary Gunship) are action buttons.
- **Your leader**: click it to open its menu: **Use action** for each of its action abilities (with what it does), **Deploy** (greyed out with the reason when you can't, such as how many resources it needs), and **See the card**.
- **Your base**: click a base with an **Epic Action**, or an action from an upgrade on it, to open its menu: **Use Epic Action** (with what it does; greyed out once it has been used, since you get it once a game), any action an upgrade on the base gives, and **See the card**.
- **Spreading damage a point at a time** (indirect damage dealt to you, or a card that splits its damage or healing): the prompt says what to do, click a highlighted unit or base for each point, and a count under your counts says how many are placed, such as **1 of 2 placed**, with **Done** when the card lets you stop early.
- **The opponent's turn**: while the opponent acts, the action buttons give way to **Opponent…**, and the prompt says what it is doing before it is done, with the unit acting ringed **amber** and its target **red**. With the opponent's pace at its fastest there is nothing to show: its reply lands at once.
- **Regroup**: the two cards you have just drawn are tagged **NEW** while you choose one to resource, and **Don't resource** keeps them all.
- **Undo**: off unless you turn it on, under **Allow undo** in **Settings**. With it on, an **Undo** button appears under the action buttons once you've taken an action (and at the foot of the log on a phone). It rewinds to just before your last move, **including the opponent's reply to it**, so one click takes back the whole exchange. You can keep clicking to rewind further, all the way back to the opening mulligan; the button disappears when there's nothing left to take back, and once the game is over. Replaying the same move gives the **same** response from the opponent, so it isn't a way to re-roll a bad outcome, but it does rewind past a mulligan, a draw or a search, which means deciding again with cards you have already seen. That is why it's a **practice tool** rather than part of a game: turn it on to try a line again, and expect a game played with it to be worth less as a record of how you played. Games remember how many times you used it, and undone moves are left out of the saved game record.
- **More** holds **Help**, **Settings**, **Report a bug** (on a computer, when it's turned on) and **Concede**, which asks you to confirm.

**Turn structure.** Players alternate single actions. When both players pass consecutively, the round moves to the **regroup phase**: anything that happens "when the regroup phase starts" resolves first, then each player draws 2, may put 1 card from hand into resources, then everything readies and a new round begins. In regroup, **click any hand card to resource it** (or Skip resourcing to resource nothing). Whoever holds the **initiative** acts first each round: take it during a round to act first in the next one (but you'll pass for the rest of the current one).

A card put into your resource zone **by a card ability** (Long Live the Empire, The Armorer) arrives **exhausted**, exactly as one you resource yourself does. It pays for something from the next round, not the action that created it, so ramp buys you a bigger future turn rather than a bigger current one. Only a card that says otherwise in its own text gives you a ready resource.

Some cards **play a card back out of a resource zone** (The Armorer, Osha), or out of the top of your deck or an opponent's resources. When one does, you get a picker showing that whole zone, with the cards you can play highlighted and the rest dimmed. **A resource you play helps pay for itself**: it is still a resource while you pay, so a 5-cost card is playable out of a zone holding exactly 5 ready resources, one of which is that card. It leaves the zone as it enters play, and a card that says so replaces it with the top card of your deck.

A card with **Smuggle** offers this same play on its own, with no picker needed: whenever one sits in your resource zone and you can afford its printed smuggle cost, "Smuggle" shows up as an ordinary move alongside Play a Card, for as long as it stays there. With **Tech** in play every one of your resources has Smuggle, so a card that prints its own may show two Smuggle moves at different prices. **First Light** also costs 4 damage to a friendly unit, so it shows one Smuggle move for each unit that could take it. When **Lando Calrissian** plays a card using Smuggle, the same moves appear at 2 less.

**Winning.** Deal damage to the opponent's base until it reaches its health before they do the same to you. Each base shows a **large number over the card**: by default the **damage it has taken**, counting up to its printed health (which varies from base to base), or the **health remaining** counting down if you've switched **Base health** in **Settings**. If a single action defeats **both** bases at once, the game is a **draw**. If your deck runs out, drawing deals 3 damage to your own base per missed card, so don't dawdle.

**Conceding.** On your turn, **Concede** under **More** ends the game as your loss, after asking you to confirm. The opponent can concede too, but it **offers** rather than walking away: when its loss is certain, meaning every move it could make leaves you able to finish its base with a single attack on your next action, it offers to concede, once a game. **Accept** to take the win, or **Decline** to play it out; it won't ask again that game. The game-over screen says when a game ended by concession.

**Keywords, upgrades and card abilities.** The rules engine runs the cards, not just the board:

- **Keywords** apply: Sentinel (enemies **in the same arena** must attack it, so a unit able to reach across arenas is not forced by a Sentinel in the other one; a unit with Sentinel can always be attacked, even if a card says it can't be), Saboteur (ignores Sentinel and defeats the defender's Shields before it attacks), Raid, Grit, Overwhelm, Restore, **Ambush** (may attack the moment it enters play; declining leaves it exhausted, as a played unit normally enters, unless it would have entered ready anyway, as a deployed leader does), **Shielded** (is given a Shield token when it's played or deployed), **Hidden** (can't be attacked for the rest of the phase it was played in, unless it has Sentinel), **Support** (lends its abilities to another attacker), and **Advantage**.
- **Exploit** lets you defeat some of your own units as you play the card, each making it 2 cheaper. A card with Exploit is playable whenever the most it could save would make it affordable. Click it and the game asks for your units first, before anything is paid: click each unit to exploit, then **Done**. Done appears only once what is left to pay is affordable, and reaching the card's Exploit number plays it straight away. The exploited units' *when defeated* abilities go into the same queue as the card's own *when played*, so you order them together. The Marauder works the same way, dealing 1 damage to each unit you pick and costing 1 less for each. Greater Sarlacc does it with your ready resources instead: pick each resource to defeat from the menu, and it costs 3 less for each one.
- **Upgrades** attach to a unit, yours *or* the enemy's, and modify power/health, grant keywords, or add abilities. **Fortify** upgrades attach to your own base instead, never a unit.
- **Piloting**: a Pilot card can be played as a unit, or for its **Piloting** cost as an upgrade on one of your **Vehicles without a Pilot** (one Pilot per Vehicle unless a card says otherwise). Clicking the card plays it as a unit; each way to play it as a Pilot is a **Play … as a Pilot** action button, naming the cost and the Vehicle. As an upgrade it adds the +X/+Y printed beside its own stats and only what its text gives the "attached unit": its own keywords and unit abilities stay behind. "When played as an upgrade" fires only for the Pilot play, "When played as a unit" only for the unit play. A defeated Pilot goes to its owner's discard like any upgrade. Some cards move between the two once in play: a unit that attaches itself to a Vehicle (Poe Dameron, Sidon Ithano, L3-37 instead of being defeated, Phantom II onto The Ghost) loses its upgrades and damage, and a Pilot moved to the ground arena as a unit (Eject, Luke Skywalker instead of being defeated) arrives exhausted. Luke always takes that move.
- **Pilot leaders**: a leader with the Pilot trait and a printed +X/+Y (most JTL leaders) can deploy as a unit or as an upgrade on one of your Vehicles without a Pilot: each Vehicle is a **Deploy … as a Pilot on …** button beside **Deploy**. The Vehicle becomes a leader unit and gets the leader's +X/+Y and what its text gives the "attached unit"; "When deployed as an upgrade" fires only for that deploy. If the Vehicle or the leader upgrade is defeated or returned to hand, the leader goes back to your base zone exhausted, as a defeated leader unit does, and cannot deploy again (Poe Dameron's own action can still flip him onto a Vehicle once he readies).
- **Playing from a discard pile**: a card that lets you play a card out of a discard pile ("for this phase, you may play that card from your discard pile"), and a card whose own **Action** works from your discard pile (Kylo's TIE Silencer, Salvaged Blaster, Brutal Traditions), each shows as a **Play … from your discard pile** action button, naming the cost and, for an upgrade, the unit it goes on. "Discarded from your hand or deck" means exactly that: a defeated unit or a played event reaches the pile without being discarded.
- **Looking at the top card of your deck**: while a card says you may look at the top card of your deck **at any time** (Hondo Ohnaka in play, or Intelligence Agency on your base), your **DECK** count opens it. **Click it** to see the top card, as often as you like and on either player's turn, and close it to put it away. Looking is not a move: it costs nothing, uses no action, adds nothing to the log, and your opponent is not told what the card is or that you looked. The count stops opening when the card that allowed it leaves play or loses its abilities.
- **Grand Admiral Thrawn's look**: Thrawn prints "When the action phase starts: Look at the top card of each player's deck" on both sides. If he is your leader, each round opens with both cards shown centre-screen, yours and your opponent's, each labelled with whose deck it is on. It is free and there is nothing to choose: press **Done**, or click beside the cards, and the round begins. You see them at that moment only, not later in the round. It is private: the log records that you finished looking and never which cards they were, and when your opponent's Thrawn looks, nothing is shown on your side. A deck with no cards left shows nothing, and if both are empty you are not asked. When both players lead with Thrawn you are first asked whose ability resolves first, as for any two abilities that trigger together.
- **Unique** cards (star by the name) are one-per-player. Come to control a second copy of a unique **upgrade** or **unit** and you choose one of the two to defeat: pick the upgrade in the centre-screen prompt, or click the duplicate unit on the board. (Your opponent controlling their own copy is fine, as the limit is per player.)
- **Card abilities** resolve, including triggered effects (**when played**, **when defeated**, **when an attack ends**, which still fires even if the attacker was defeated in the combat, **when a unit readies**, **on attack/defence**), activated **Action** abilities, and the interactive choices they raise (pay-or-exhaust, play a revealed card free, deal damage, search-and-discard, an optional follow-up attack, and so on).
- **Leader abilities**: some leaders have an ability you use while they're **undeployed** (click your leader card, then a highlighted target). Once **deployed**, the leader is a unit on the board with its own (usually stronger) ability, keywords and stats.

Every upgrade **and every leader** (both sides) in the ASH set is implemented, and so are most leaders from the other sets. A leader whose ability isn't built yet still plays with its printed stats and keywords. Some leaders say **When Deployed**: that ability fires as the leader unit arrives. Cards whose ability text isn't built still make fine resources.

## The board

The game is laid out the same way on a phone and on a computer, larger on a computer. On a phone, play with it **on its side**: held upright, the game asks you to **turn your phone sideways**, and carries on where it was when you do.

### Arenas, bases and leaders

- **The controls column** down the left holds, from the top: **Back** (leave the game), the round and phase, and **More**; the **opponent's counts** (resources ready of total, hand, deck, discard); the **prompt**, saying what the board is waiting for; on a computer, the **log**; **your counts**; and the **action buttons** (Mulligan and Keep hand, Take the initiative and Pass, Undo when it's on). On a phone the log has a **Log** button of its own beside More. **INITIATIVE**, **FORCE** and **CRED n** sit beside the heading of whoever holds them.
- **The battlefield** fills the rest: **Space** on the left and **Ground** on the right, each in two panels, the opponent's above and yours below, with both bases and leaders in the strip between them. Reading down the middle you'll find the opponent's leader, the opponent's base, your base, then your leader, so the two bases meet at the battlefront. Units line up along the battlefront, both front rows meeting in the middle, and **Sentinels** are held at the front.
- **Your hand** runs under the board: playable cards are ringed **blue** when a click will **play** them (action phase), **green** when a click will **resource** them (setup and regroup), the rest dimmed, and a long hand fans so it all fits. A card's shown cost includes any **aspect penalty** (+2 per aspect icon your leader and base don't provide).
- **Piles**: click a **DISC** count to see that discard pile. When a card lets you look at the top of your deck, click your **DECK** count.

### Damage, tokens and badges

- **Units**: each unit is shown as its **card art** in a square slot, four to a row (five once a side holds more than eight), turned sideways when exhausted, with its power and health in its bottom corners and a badge for what is on it: damage in the middle, **Shd** and **Exp** for Shield and Experience tokens, **+n** for upgrade cards, **Sentinel**, **Hidden** (Sentinel overrides Hidden: a hidden unit that gains Sentinel becomes attackable), and **LEADER** on a deployed leader, whose slot in the strip says **Deployed**. The power and health shown are what the unit has now, with every upgrade, token, aura and "this phase" boost counted. A unit guarding captured cards carries an amber **n CAPT** tag above it: tap the tag to see what it holds, whose card each is, and that it comes back into play exhausted for its owner when the guard leaves play. If a card's art can't be loaded, it shows a **text summary** instead (cost, name, power/HP, keywords and abilities).
- **Upgrades & tokens**: **Shield** soaks the next instance of damage then pops, **Experience** is +1/+1 per token, **Advantage** is +1/0 on its next attack or defence, and **Weakness** is -1/-1 per token (a unit it takes to 0 health is defeated). A Shield or Advantage token that gets spent is **defeated**, exactly as a card upgrade is, so cards that watch for a defeated upgrade (Zeb Orrelios, Baylan Skoll) react to it. A "this phase" boost clears at the end of the phase (a unit only that boost was keeping alive is then defeated); an aura lasts while its source is in play. A defeated card-upgrade returns to its **owner's** discard (which may not be the unit's controller); tokens simply vanish. An upgrade with **Fortify** goes on **your base** instead of a unit: its name is shown along the foot of the base card, and whatever it says the base gains works for you while it's there. A card that defeats or returns "an upgrade" can take one on a base (one that says "on a unit" cannot): when choosing an upgrade to defeat, click the base, then the upgrade.

### Target colours

A ring hugs a card when it's selectable, a target, or a playable hand card. A target's colour says what the effect will do to it: **green** is good for it (a buff, a heal, a Shield, Experience or Advantage token, readying it), **red** is bad for it (damage, a debuff, a Weakness token, defeat, or an attack), **yellow** is something cunning (exhausting, capturing, returning it to hand, taking control of it, or picking which of your units does the deed), and **white** attaches an upgrade card to it, which you judge for yourself. Bases take the same colours: attacking the opponent's base is red, healing your own is green.

### The Force and Credit tokens

A **Credit token** (**CRED n**, with how many are held) is spare money: while you hold any, **anything that costs resources asks how you want to pay first**, whether that is a card from your hand, a card played from somewhere else (Smuggle, a discard pile, your resources), a leader's or a unit's action, or a "you may pay" a card offers. The prompt names what you are paying for and how much. Set how many Credit tokens to defeat with **−** and **+** (each pays 1), and it shows what is left for your resources; **Pay** pays it all at once. The count starts at the fewest your ready resources allow, which is none when they cover the whole cost, because it is your call which to spend. The tokens are defeated as you press Pay, and **cancelling** the prompt takes the whole action back with nothing spent. A card that lets **units pay as resources** (Vuutun Palaa's Droids) asks on the board instead: click a highlighted unit to exhaust it for 1, one at a time, then pay the rest with a **Defeat n Credit tokens to pay** button or **Done**. A unit can't pay for its own action this way.

**FORCE** shows that the Force is with that player: a player has one or none, a card that says "the Force is with you" creates it, and a card that says "use the Force" defeats it to get its effect. Both are public, so the opponent's show too.

### Seeing a card and the log

- **Seeing a card**: **tap and hold** any card (in your hand, on the board, a leader or a base) to see it full size; with a mouse, hold **Shift** over it. A unit in play shows what is on it beside the card: its damage and the HP it has left, whether it is ready and in which arena, and its upgrades and tokens by name. Beyond that are the cards on it, each shown as a card: its upgrade cards, and any cards it holds captive with whose each is and that it comes back into play exhausted for its owner when the guard leaves play. When they don't all fit, that row scrolls. Tap anywhere to close it. Holding a card never plays it. While a leader is zoomed with Shift, hold **Alt** to see its other (unit) side.
- **Card names**: any card named in the **log** or in a **prompt** is shown in **bold and underlined**, coloured **blue** if you control it and **amber** if the opponent does. Tap it to see the card, or hover it with a mouse, no Shift needed.
- **Card printings**: a card can be printed several ways (Normal, Hyperspace, foil, prestige, showcase), each with its own collector number, and your imported deck keeps whichever you own. They all play identically: the game matches your printing to the card it is, so abilities work whatever version you opened, while the board still shows **your** art. A card reprinted in a **later set** is the same card too, and plays the same way. If a printing can't be identified (you're offline, say, and that set has never been cached), the card still plays but only with its **printed stats and keywords**, and a notice over the top of the board names exactly which cards are affected until you dismiss it with its **×**.
- **Log**: a running record of every action, newest first, under the round each action was taken in, each action in one row with what it changed beneath it: damage and healing on units and bases, upgrades and tokens gained or lost, units entering play, being defeated, leaving play or changing sides, and cards going to a discard pile. They say what happened, not which card caused it, and when one action does several things they are listed together. Damage that only lands once a later choice is answered is listed under that answer. Anything revealed or discarded is named. **Hidden cards are never named**: what your opponent draws, resources, holds in hand, or moves around their deck shows as "a card" or not at all. Undoing an action takes its lines with it. On a computer it is in the controls column; on a phone **Log** slides it in from the left over the controls, leaving the board in view.

## Prompts

- **What am I being asked?**: the **prompt** in the controls column says what the board is waiting for ("choose a unit to deal 2 damage to"), and where the card that caused it is known, it leads with that card. This matters most when the **opponent's** card is what triggered the decision. It also covers the steps that aren't resolved by clicking the board: the **mulligan** and **resourcing**, both during setup, counting off the two cards you must pick, and at the regroup step where it's optional. Otherwise it says what clicking does.
- **A card asks you something**: a panel opens over the dimmed board with the card that asked (tap it to see it full size), its question and the answers, the one it expects in blue. A choice you answer by clicking the board, such as **Ambush** or another "attack right now", stays on the board instead, with **Don't ambush** (or the decline) in the action buttons. **Who resolves first** lists both sides' abilities, with **They resolve first** and **I resolve first**; **which resolves next** lists your waiting abilities, one button each. A card that lets you look at the top of your deck shows it beside the question, which only you see. When a card asks you to **pick cards** (a search, a discard pile, your resources, or the opponent's hand), the ones you can choose are ringed and the rest dimmed: tap one to choose it, hold any of them to read it, and **Done** when you have finished. A search **always shows you the cards it looked at, even when none of them match**: they are about to go to the bottom of your deck, and knowing which ones is worth having. **Naming a card**: type part of the name, tap the match, then **Name** it.
- **Cancelling**: when the panel came from **your own** move, the **×** in its corner **cancels that move**, as if you had never made it: the card goes back to your hand, the resources come back, and you can do something else. It isn't offered once the move has shown you anything hidden (a draw, a search, a look at a hand or the top of a deck) or drawn something random, because going back then would let you decide again knowing the cards, and it isn't offered for anything the **opponent's** card asked of you. On the opponent's prompts **Look at the board** puts the panel aside instead; the prompt says it is waiting and **Show choice** brings it back. The other prompts on the opponent's turn stay until you answer them.
- **When both of you have abilities waiting** (say two units trade in combat and each has a *when defeated* ability), a centre-screen prompt asks **who resolves first**. It lists both sides' waiting abilities and the card each came from, and you pick **I resolve first** or **They resolve first**. It's your call because you're the player taking the action, and it's the only thing you choose: how the *opponent* orders their own abilities is up to them. Nothing else can be clicked until you answer, because the order changes what the later abilities find, though when your own move set the abilities off its **×** cancels the move (see **Cancelling**).
- **When several of your own abilities trigger at once**, a second prompt asks **which resolves next**, with one button per waiting ability, each naming the card it came from. That matters more than it sounds: a single unit often carries two, its own ability and one granted by an upgrade attached to it, and the card name is the only thing telling them apart. Where two of the buttons would read identically (two copies of the same card, each with the same ability) they're numbered instead. There's no way to decline *here*, because all of them will resolve; the only question is the sequence. **Turning one down happens later**, on the ability's own prompt when its turn comes: anything worded "you may" arrives with a Decline (or "Don't") button, and anything that isn't has nothing to decline.
- **The order you pick is real.** Each ability finishes completely before the next starts, so an earlier one changes what the later ones find: deploy a leader first and it's a friendly unit that the next ability can target, clear a lane first and the attack that follows has different options. That's why the game asks rather than choosing for you. It doesn't ask when there's nothing to decide: one ability firing several times off a single event (a unit losing three upgrades at once), or an ability whose "if…" condition isn't met at that moment.
- **Shielded and Ambush are ordered like any other ability.** Play a unit with both while there's an enemy unit it can attack, and the **which resolves next** prompt offers "Give a Shield token to this unit" and "You may attack with this unit". Take the attack first and the unit takes the hit, then gets its Shield, which it keeps; take the Shield first and the Shield soaks the hit and is gone. A unit with Grit, like Mae, usually wants the attack first: the damage is power, and the Shield is still to come. Whatever you leave for later waits until the whole attack is over. The same goes for a Shielded unit whose other ability could do something with the Shield, and for a leader that deploys with both. You aren't asked when the order couldn't show: a Shielded unit with nothing else to resolve, an Ambush with nothing to attack, or an ability that ends up in the same place either way (healing your base, giving a token to a different unit) all just get their Shield first.
- **An unmet "if…" gets a second look.** Abilities that can do something go first, and the board is checked again afterwards, so one of them can switch another on. Play Luke Skywalker with two units out and his "if you control at least 4 units" is short by one, until you take Grogu's deploy, which makes four and lets Luke's sweep fire. He isn't spent while the condition is still unmet.
- **Ambush and Support wait their turn too.** Both are abilities that trigger on playing the unit, so they queue up with everything else that play set off, and you choose where they go in the sequence. A unit whose *when played* clears the way can do that **before** it takes its Ambush attack.
- **"Instead" abilities ask at the moment they apply.** Queen Amidala's offer to defeat another friendly unit sharing a trait with her comes as the damage is about to land, mid-combat included: one button per unit you could defeat, or **Take the damage**, and the attack carries on once you answer. Vice Admiral Rampart asks whenever an upgrade on your base would be defeated, even by your own card's cost or by the opponent's card: defeat him and the upgrade stays, and whatever defeated it still goes ahead. Ty Yorrick's "plus 1" is applied for you without asking: to your abilities' damage to an opponent's units and base, never to your own side.
- **Winning ends everything immediately.** If a base hits 0 while abilities are still waiting, they're discarded unresolved and you go straight to the result. A card that would have been revealed or played simply isn't.

## After the game

**Game over** shows over the board: who won and how (the base destroyed, and in which round, or who conceded), your deck and the one it played against, and **Rematch** (fresh shuffle, same decks) or **New setup**, back to choosing the decks. The controls column stays uncovered, so the log is still to hand. Completed games are recorded on-device for future analysis.

**Improving the bot.** The AI bot gets better by learning from real games. If you opt in, each game you finish is sent to train it.

**What is sent:** both decks as loaded, every decision made in the game by you and the bot, who won, how long it took, and the app version.

**What is not sent:** your name, your deck names, or anything else that identifies you. There is no account, and your IP address is not linked to games. Because of that, a game cannot be traced back to you, and it also cannot be found and deleted later.

This data export is disabled unless you turn it on, and you can change it at any time in Settings.

Turning it off stops future games being sent and does not recall games already sent.

**Reporting a bug**: off unless you turn it on, under **Report a bug button** in **Settings**, and only on a computer. With it on, **Report a bug** under **More** opens a short form for a title and what happened. **Copy report and open GitHub** copies a full report to your clipboard and opens GitHub's new-issue page with the title already filled in and the body left empty, so all you do is **paste and submit**. The issue is filed under **your own GitHub account**, so you need to be signed in there. The report carries the whole game so far, so the problem can be replayed exactly rather than guessed at, along with the build number and whether you were on dev or prod. If the clipboard can't be reached, the form stays open with the report shown for you to copy by hand.
